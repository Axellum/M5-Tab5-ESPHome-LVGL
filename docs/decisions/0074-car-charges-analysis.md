# ADR-0074: Car charges analysed by Home Assistant from the recorder — charges, solar share, kWh per hour and month

**Status:** Proposed (2026-11-09; the Home Assistant half of the cars in the Energy popup — built and tested off-device, never run on a real Home Assistant nor with a real car).
**Date:** 2026-11-09

## Context

A user with a solar installation and two electric cars (Tesla, through the Tesla Fleet or Teslemetry integration of Home Assistant; [discussion #278](https://github.com/Axellum/M5-Tab5-ESPHome-LVGL/discussions/278)) wants the cars in the Energy popup ([ADR-0028](0028-solar-energy-popup.md), [ADR-0058](0058-energy-sun-forecast-balance.md)): the last charges and how much of each came from the sun, the kWh per hour of a day, the month's balance. The integrations give the battery level, the charger power and the energy added of the current charge — **none of these**: no list of charges, no share of the sun, no kWh per hour, no month. Home Assistant has to compute them, from its own data, the way the Energy popup does ([ADR-0062](0062-energy-balance-kwh-grid-battery.md)).

The firmware never calls a Home Assistant action ([ADR-0025](0025-events-only.md)) and the public packages hold no entity of a real home ([ADR-0024](0024-packages-without-placeholders.md)): the sensors are handed to the script by the blueprint, as for `script.tab5_energie`.

## Decision

- **A script that only reads and answers.** `script.tab5_voitures_analyse` (`packages/tab5_voitures.yaml`) takes `capteurs` (solar power sensors, the home power sensor, whether it counts the cars, one or two cars: power of charge, optional odometer and energy added) and an optional `jour`, reads `recorder.get_statistics` and returns its result as the script response. It pushes nothing to the tablet and calls no service of the car's integration, so it never wakes the car. A later lot pushes the strings it returns.
- **Statistics, not states.** The charger power (`state_class: measurement`) is read as its mean, in W (`units: {power: W}`: Home Assistant converts the kW the Tesla sensors give). The 5-minute rows are kept `purge_keep_days` days (10 by default); older ones only exist hourly. The two are stitched into one series of slots of 300 s or 3600 s: hourly rows before the first full hour covered by 5-minute rows, 5-minute rows after. Charges, shares and hours work the same on both; the precision does not (the package README says how).
- **A charge** is a run of slots whose mean power is above **100 W**; slots separated by **30 minutes** at most are one charge (a data hole, a pause under a cloud). Duration runs from the first to the last slot; energy is the sum of power × duration. The ends of a charge are known to 5 minutes, or to the hour on hourly rows (the duration then counts whole hours, up to two too many; the energy stays right).
- **Share of the sun**, slot by slot: *available* = solar − home consumption without the cars, never below 0 (the cars' charge is subtracted from the home sensor when it counts them, the default); each car receives the fraction `min(1, available / charge of all the cars)` of its power. The share of a charge is Σ solar / Σ energy over the slots where it can be known, 0 to 100 %. Where the plan said `min(charge, available)` per car, this version divides the available sun between cars charging together: two cars at 4 kW with 5.2 kW available get 65 % each, not 100 % each (which would count 8 kW of sun out of 5.2). With one car the two formulas are the same. A slot without a home row is left out of both sums; solar sensors without a row count for 0 (an inverter off at night is a charge at 0 %). No home sensor: the share is `nan` (« -- »); no solar sensor: it is empty (not chosen).
- **Month**: from the 1st at 00:00 to now, same slots. kWh/100 km is the energy charged divided by the kilometres of the month (odometer statistics, `change`, in km), only above 20 km, and is an approximation — a charge is not a consumption (the previous day's charge, charging away from home). The share of the house is the cars' energy over the home sensor's energy of the month.
- **Formats** (plan §5, family of `tab5_maj_energie*`: fields `|`, lists `;`, empty = not chosen, `nan` = no value): charges `rang|jour|heure|duree_min|kwh|part_soleil_pct` (`AAAA-MM-JJ` and `HH:MM` local, 6 lines at most, newest first, one list per car and one for both); a day's 24 kWh per local hour, empty where the car has no row; the month as 11 fields (per car: kWh, share, km, kWh/100 km, kWh taken from the grid; then the cars' part of the house). A `precision` field says which statistics were found.
- **One method, two writings.** `tools/voitures_charges.py` is the reference, in Python, tested by hand-computed series (night charge 0 %, midday ≈ 100 %, a cloud, a car plugged in without charging, data holes, one then several solar sensors, the home counting the cars or not, two cars together, hourly rows only, both, a daylight-saving day, the month). The Jinja of the package does the same operations in the same order and `tests/test_voitures.py` renders it in Jinja's sandbox on the same series: the strings must be identical.

## Consequences

- No contract change, no firmware change, no new entity: only a script in a new package (picked up by the release archive with the other packages).
- The calculation walks thousands of rows in Home Assistant's event loop (0.3 s on a PC for 10 000 rows; not measured on a Raspberry Pi): call it when the popup opens, then at most every five minutes.
- Charging away from home (a public charger the car reports) is counted in the lists and the month; its share of the sun is meaningless. A home battery is not counted as sun.
- Not verified on a real Home Assistant, nor with a real Tesla: the key `distance` of `units`, what the car's sensors do when it sleeps, whether `charger power` has statistics on the user's integration (the sensor must have `state_class: measurement`).
